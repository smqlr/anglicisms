Anglicisms Overview · Theory & The Problem

Anglicisms in Russian and Kazakh

How globalization and the IT sector are changing the way we speak. An anglicism is a word or phrase borrowed from English into another language. We hear and use words like like, deadline, cringe, feedback, content every day, often without even noticing that they are English.

1. Loanword

Taken straight from English, keeping form and meaning: computer, business, online.

2. Calque

Translated part by part into a native phrase: brainstorm → mozgovoy shturm.

3. Code-switching

Mixing languages inside one sentence: "Skin' feedback", "Bul óte cringe".

Main Research Question (The Problem)

“Do anglicisms enrich our languages, or do they push native words out?”

Student Survey Results Dashboard

Empirical analysis of how students aged 15–17 use and perceive anglicisms in Russian and Kazakh.

📋 Platform: Google Forms 🌐 Bilingual: Kazakh & Russian 👥 Respondents: Ages 15–17 (17–20 students)
Usage Frequency 11 of 17 respondents
65%

use English words in speech at least sometimes

Only 2–3 respondents use them "often", showing that borrowing is mostly situational rather than total.

Attitudes Toward Anglicisms Opinions Split
41% believe they enrich

another 41% see no effect, and 18% think they harm the language.

82% of surveyed students view anglicisms as either beneficial or neutral in daily communication.

Replacing With Native Words Key Finding
47%

would replace an anglicism only where a native word exists

35% say replacement is unnecessary, while 18% support replacing borrowed words in all contexts.
